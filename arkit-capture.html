One unknown, and iOS knows it

The published trajectory can't name a speed. It gives a family: 44 km/h at f = 500, 105 km/h at f = 1300. Not because the physics is weak, but because a single camera cannot recover its own focal length from a goal viewed head-on — the rectangle projects identically at every focal-and-depth pair.

ARKit publishes the exact intrinsics per frame. fx, fy, cx, cy, measured by the device that took the picture. The unknown doesn't get estimated better — it stops being unknown.

The identity, before and after

Today's measurements split into two classes. Lateral and vertical are focal-free, because the focal cancels:

X = (x − cx) · D / d     ← f cancels. Already true metres.

Depth does not cancel, and that one term poisons every speed:

Z = f · D / d     ← f unknown ⟹ a family, not a number

With ARKit intrinsics, f is simply read from the frame. The family collapses. Nothing else about the pipeline has to change — the drag model, the tracker, the anchors all stay exactly as they are.

The LiDAR trap — and the way around it

LiDAR cannot measure the goal from where you shoot

Effective range is ~5 m (up to 10 m on iPhone 15 Pro and later), and direct sunlight saturates the infrared band and washes returns out entirely. A goal 33 m away on a sunny pitch is doubly out of reach. Any plan that points LiDAR at a distant goal fails.

The way around it is that ARKit's world frame is the tape measure, not LiDAR's range. Visual-inertial odometry maintains a gravity-aligned metric coordinate system as you walk. So:

1. Stand at the goal. LiDAR scans the posts at 2–3 m → true dimensions ±1–3 cm, and drops a world anchor on the goal line. 2. Walk back to the spot. ARKit tracks the walk. The goal anchor stays fixed in the world frame — metrically, the whole way. 3. Shoot. Camera pose is now known RELATIVE TO THE GOAL, in metres, with gravity down and f known per frame.

Nothing has to see the goal from 33 m. The distance is carried in the world frame across the walk. This is what ARKit is genuinely excellent at, and it sidesteps the range limit completely.

The risk this plan lives or dies on: VIO drift

Walking ~33 m and back accumulates tracking drift. If that drift is a few centimetres, the trajectory becomes exact. If it's a metre, the anchor is worthless and we're back to guessing. Nobody has measured this on a pitch. It is measurable in an afternoon, and it decides whether the rest of this plan is worth building — which is why it is Phase 0 and why nothing else starts until it passes.

Second trap: intrinsics move

ARCamera.intrinsics changes per frame, because autofocus physically moves the lens and refocusing changes the effective focal length. Recording one K at the start and applying it to the whole clip reintroduces exactly the error we set out to remove — quietly, and in the direction that looks fine.

Two defences, use both: lock focus before the strike (AVCaptureDevice .setFocusModeLocked), and log K on every frame anyway. If a locked clip shows K drifting, that itself is the bug report.

This is the same failure shape that has bitten this project repeatedly: a default value read as a measurement. A single K applied to 30 frames is a default wearing a measurement's clothes.

Build plan

0

Prove the world anchor survives the walk

~2 days · 1 phone · no pitch needed, a car park works

Minimal ARKit app: start session, drop an anchor, walk 35 m, walk back, measure how far the anchor has moved from truth. Repeat on grass, in sun, in shade. Also log whether K drifts under locked focus.

Gate: anchor error under ~10 cm at 35 m round trip. Above ~50 cm, this approach is dead and we say so rather than building on it.
1

Capture app — video plus truth

~4 days · SwiftUI + ARKit

Records 4K30 video alongside a per-frame JSONL: timestamp, 3×3 intrinsics, 4×4 camera transform, gravity vector, tracking state, and the goal anchor. One file the Python pipeline can read directly. Bundles the LiDAR goal scan from step 1 of the walk.

Deliverable: a .mp4 + .jsonl pair that makes every camera parameter in pipeline/ball.py an input rather than a fit.
2

Metric trajectory

~3 days · Python, existing code

Feed known K, known pose, known gravity and known ground plane into the existing fit. Delete the focal sweep, the tilt search and the basin ranking — all of it exists only to grope for numbers the phone now supplies. Gravity stops being a fitted nuisance and becomes a constant pointing the right way.

Gate: the speed band collapses to a single value with a real error bar, and the two rulers (ball diameter, goal width) agree within ~5%.
3

Validate against radar

~$300 + one session · 100 paired kicks

The step that converts "self-consistent" into "correct". Nothing in this pipeline has ever been checked against an instrument. Until it is, every speed is a claim.

Gate: agreement within ±3 km/h across the range, with no systematic slope — a slope means a scale error still hiding somewhere.
4

The same capture fixes match analytics

~1 week · reuses everything above

The unexpected payoff. Amateur pitch calibration currently succeeds on ~9% of frames because the keypoint model was trained on TV angles and has never seen a phone at pitch level. But if the operator walks the touchline once with ARKit, the pitch geometry is measured directly — corners, centre circle, penalty box, all as metric world anchors. No keypoint model required at all.

Why this matters: it converts the single biggest blocker on the match-analytics side from "retrain a model on footage we don't have" into "walk the touchline once before kick-off".

What this does not fix

  • Two-phone footage still has to be shot. ARKit helps calibrate each phone; it does not film the match. Real two-camera frames processed remains 0.
  • Non-Pro iPhones have no LiDAR. Intrinsics and world tracking work on all modern devices; only the goal scan needs a Pro. Fall back to assumed regulation dimensions, which projection already confirms to 0.15%.
  • Sunlight still defeats the LiDAR scan. Scan the goal in shade or on an overcast day, or accept the assumed dimensions.
  • It is a custom app. None of this is reachable from the stock Camera app — that metadata is not written to the video file. This is real iOS work, not a setting.
