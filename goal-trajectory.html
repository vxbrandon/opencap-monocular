Focal length — the one genuinely unknown quantity

The clip was re-encoded and Apple's lens metadata was stripped, so the camera's focal length is unknown. Everything you can see along the depth axis scales with it; the shape does not. The goal cannot rescue this: it is fronto-parallel in frame (post asymmetry 1.93 %, crossbar tilted 1.44°), so a rectangle projects as a rectangle at any focal/depth pair. It fixes the depth once you choose a focal — it cannot choose one.

The goal is measured, not invented

Its pixel corners were localised on a 121-frame median image, and its real-world size is confirmed by the footage itself: the projected mouth has aspect 3.0044 against the metric 3.0000 of a regulation 7.32 × 2.44 m goal — a 0.15 % agreement. This is the one solid object in the scene. (The earlier viewer on this site drew a goal at an assumed 3 × 2 m and placed it where the fit happened to end. That goal was decoration. This one is data.)

Only the mouth rectangle is measured. The netting drawn behind it is there so the frame reads as a goal.

What is not claimed

No point speed. The pipeline refuses one on this clip and so does this page — the readout above is explicitly conditional (“launch km/h if this focal”), and the honest answer is the whole family: 44 km/h at f = 500 through 105 km/h at f = 1300. The colour gradient along the path encodes relative speed so the shape reads; no individual point value is asserted.

The vertical axis is the camera's own up direction, not gravity's. The camera's height and tilt are unsolved — a separate solve on this clip puts it near ground level, tilted about 5° up. So the reference plane the shadow falls on is drawn through the goal's feet for legibility; it is not a surveyed pitch, and “height above that plane” is not height above the grass.

Depth is also the noisiest axis even at a fixed focal, because it is read off the ball's apparent diameter, which the tracker reports to the nearest half pixel. Late in the flight the ball is about 9.5 px across, so half a pixel is roughly 5 % of the depth. That is why the far end of the track wobbles toward and away from the camera in a way no struck ball does.

Timing: 30.000 fps, and why the container is wrong

Speeds were computed at 30.000 fps. The container advertises an r_frame_rate of 31.58, which disagrees with the decoded rate by about +5.3 %. Since a speed scales one-for-one with the assumed frame rate, believing the container would have inflated every speed on this page by that much. The pipeline flags exactly this: it warns when the container's two rate statements disagree by more than 2 %, and on this clip it sets point_value_allowed = false for that reason alone.

Depth component = the along-axis part of the launch velocity, 0.02196 m/s per pixel of focal. The lateral 5.37 m/s and vertical 0.66 m/s add to it in quadrature and never change, which is why the speed column compresses at the low end.
