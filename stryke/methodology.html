STRYKE · measurement methodology

How We
Measure

Every number the app shows, where it comes from, and — just as important — every number it refuses to show and why. If you only read one section, read the last one.

01Distance: the goal is the ruler

A camera sees pixels. To get metres you need one object in frame whose real size you already know. That object is the goal.

goal mouth = 7.32 m × 2.44 m (regulation, declared) detected in-frame → posts + crossbar → 4 image points solvePnP(object points, image points, K) → depth in metres

Every distance in the app descends from that one solve. It is why the whole goal must be in frame — with a post cut off there are no four corners, no PnP, and no metres.

The subtlety that bitesWhen the goal faces the camera dead-on, the rectangle projects as a rectangle at any focal/depth pair — PnP cannot separate them. The code detects this (goal_fronto_parallel) and falls back to scale-only depth rather than pretending the pose is solved. It's another reason to shoot from 30–60° off the shot line.

02Focal length: measured, not guessed

PnP needs the camera's focal length. Until recently the pipeline inferred it from the image diagonal and tagged the result focal_from_diagonal_heuristic_pm15pct — a ±15% error that transfers 1:1 into every distance and speed.

clip recorded in-app → sidecar carries the real intrinsic matrix K = [[fx, s, cx], [0, fy, cy], [0, 0, 1]] ← from AVFoundation, per clip camera.source = "device_intrinsics" (was: "diagonal_heuristic")

A clip that arrives without a sidecar still works — it just keeps the ±15% warning and the wider band. That is the honest cost of an imported clip.

03Speed: why it is a band

speed = distance ÷ time

Two independent rulers, two independent errors. The metres come from the goal solve; the seconds come from the video's frame clock. Speed scales 1:1 with frame rate — a clock wrong by 30% gives a speed wrong by 30%.

So the app fits the flight, propagates both uncertainties, and publishes the interval that covers the truth — not the midpoint dressed as a fact.

04Why a point speed gets refused

This is the mechanism people ask about most. A screen recording of a video carries a broken clock, and the file says so three ways at once:

SignalValueWhat it means
duplicate_frame_ratio0.41642% of frames are the same picture repeated
avg_frame_rate vs r_frame_rate57.06 vs 75.0the container contradicts itself
retimed_suspectedtruere-timed after recording

A file has one true frame rate. This one claims two, 31% apart. Picking either would be inventing the answer, so the point value is withheld and the band stands.

Proof that the refusal is rightWe stripped the duplicates and rebuilt a clean 30 fps clock. The pipeline then produced 154.8 km/h — absurd for a free kick whose goal-anchored estimate is ~60. That is exactly what papering over a broken clock buys you.

06The keeper: geometry, never probability

"Would a pro have saved it?" is a probability question, and we have no measured distribution of keeper behaviour. So the app answers a geometry question instead, which it can actually defend:

t_available = time from contact to the goal plane t_move = t_available − REACTION_TIME_S reach = STANDING_HAND_REACH + ∫ dive velocity dt (capped at peak) margin = |crossing − keeper start| − reach verdict = OUTSIDE / INSIDE envelope / INDETERMINATE

The envelope is a disc — isotropic, knowingly generous to the keeper, so an "outside" verdict is the conservative one. Below tier B, or on an untrusted clock, it returns INDETERMINATE rather than a verdict.

07Comparison: overlapping bands are not a difference

Comparing two bands is where a lot of sports apps quietly lie. If your band is 43–83 and your last session was 47–86, you did not get worse — those intervals overlap and the honest answer is that they're indistinguishable.

disjoint(A, B) → direction + separation, with both intervals as evidence overlapping → INDISTINGUISHABLE fewer than N shots → the gate, not a verdict

Four dimensions — speed, placement, distance, consistency — get four verdicts. There is deliberately no single composite score: "indistinguishable on all four" is a complete answer.

09The one thing that changes everything

Ground truthAlmost every gate above unlocks with the same key: a radar gun in frame for ten shots. Until a measured reference exists, the app can only publish intervals it can defend. With one, the band becomes a calibrated instrument and the point value, the percentiles and the ranks all become answerable.

Everything else in this document is engineering. That one is a purchase.
