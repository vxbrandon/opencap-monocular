How to get the app onto your iPhone, what to film, and exactly why each rule exists. Follow the geometry rules and the app can measure; break them and it will honestly refuse.

1 · Install on your iPhone

Today  No Apple account needed

Plug the iPhone into the Mac, unlock it, and trust the computer. Then:

cd ~/Desktop/programming/stryke/apps/fe-mobile
npx expo run:ios --device

Pick your phone from the list. At the signing prompt choose your personal team — a free Apple ID works. First build takes ~10 minutes.

Then on the iPhone: Settings → General → VPN & Device Management → trust your developer certificate. The app lasts 7 days, then re-run the command.

Why this mattersThe simulator has no camera. This is the step that turns STRYKE from a demo into a measuring instrument.

Later  TestFlight

Needs the Apple Developer Program ($99/yr). Full steps are in docs/RELEASE.md; the EAS project is already wired as @aqin_tbg/stryke.

2 · Camera position — the rules that decide everything

01
The whole goal must be in frame, all four corners. The goal is the ruler. Its known 7.32 m × 2.44 m frame is what converts pixels into metres. If a post is cut off or hidden behind the keeper, there is no scale and every number degrades to a guess.
02
Stand to the side of the shot line, 30–60° off it. Straight behind the ball is the worst angle: the ball travels almost directly away from the lens, so its motion is nearly invisible and depth becomes ambiguous. From the side the flight sweeps across the frame and the solve is well-conditioned.
03
The phone must not move. Lean it on a bag, a bottle, a fence — anything rigid. A pan of a few degrees is enough to corrupt the scale: a real panning clip produced a confident 5.8–9.0 km/h for a struck free kick. The app reads the gyroscope and will now refuse rather than lie, but a still phone is what actually gets you a measurement.
04
Ball at rest, in frame, ~2 seconds before contact. The solver needs to see the ball sitting still to lock onto it and to know exactly when contact happened. A ball that enters frame already moving loses the launch point.
05
Landscape, 1× lens, no zoom. Any zoom invalidates the recorded lens calibration. Stay on the main wide camera.
06
Bright enough to freeze the ball. Low light means long exposure means a smeared ball, and a smeared ball has no precise centre. Daylight or good floodlights; avoid shooting into the sun or a floodlight.
07
Don't stop the recording between shots. Let it run through a set. The app cuts each shot out itself and keeps the timing intact.

3 · What the app records for you automatically

You don't have to do anything for these — but they are the reason a phone recording beats a screen recording by a wide margin.

SignalWhat it fixes
Camera intrinsics
the lens's true focal length		Today the solver guesses focal length from the image diagonal — a ±15% error that lands directly on every distance and speed. The iPhone hands us the real matrix on every frame.
Per-frame timestampsTurns the time base from inferred into measured. This is the single gate standing between a band and a point speed.
Gyroscope / attitudeMeasures exactly how much the phone rotated, so small motion can be compensated and large motion honestly refused instead of silently corrupting the scale.
Exposure & ISORecords how much motion blur to expect, so the ball-centre uncertainty is real rather than assumed.

4 · What to bring back from the pitch

Set A · the baseline10 free kicks from ~18–20 m, same spot, same ball, phone still and side-on. This measures repeatability — how tight the numbers are for shots that were genuinely similar. Stability is measured, not claimed.
Set B · the distance sweep3 shots each from 8 m, 12 m, 16 m, 20 m, 25 m. Error usually grows with distance; this tells us where the honest limit is.
Set C · the angle sweep3 shots each with the phone at 30°, 45°, 60°, and one deliberately from behind the ball. The last one should come back refused — that's the test.
Set D · the stress setA few clips that break the rules on purpose: phone hand-held, a post out of frame, dusk light. These prove the refusals fire instead of producing confident nonsense.
The one thing that unlocks a real number: ground truthA Pocket Radar PR1000-BC (~$300) beside the ball, filmed in the same frame so its display is readable. Without a trusted reference we can never validate a point speed — only bands. Ten shots with the radar visible turns "43–83 km/h" into a calibrated instrument. This is the week-1 purchase.
Bonus if you have a second phoneFilm the same shots from a second angle. Two independent solves of one flight is the cheapest cross-check there is — no extra hardware.
