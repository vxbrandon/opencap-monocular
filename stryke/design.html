v5 — the production look. Thought log.

Dated entries, never rewritten. Continues docs/design/v3/00-THOUGHT-LOG.md.

2026-09-14 11:20 — the founder's verdict, and what it actually names

The brief, verbatim: "generate better 3d rendering model (blender or whatever) to improve the app design" and "use very clean icons and spacing to improve the ui/ux of the app. Right now it's so AI LIKE and not production grade."

Four waves of v3/v4 made the app HONEST (735 tests, ten screens passing the cover-the-Korean test) and made it CONSISTENT (one icon set, one axis grammar). Neither made it look like a product. "AI-like" is a specific look and it is worth naming its parts, because each part was a decision this record took on purpose and can now take back on purpose.

The tells, read off docs/design/v3/final/*.png

In the chrome (my lane):

  1. Light pools under buttons. v4 called the lime CTA "a lit object" and put a soft lime ellipse under it and under the tab-bar disc. No shipped product does this — Strava, Nike Run Club, Whoop, Apple Fitness, Oura all use a flat fill. A glow under a button is the single most recognisable Dribbble/AI mannerism. Delete LightPool from every control.
  2. Two lime calls on one screen. Home has a full-width lime CTA and, 40 pt below it, a lifted lime disc in the tab bar. Same colour, same verb, twice. The disc stays (it is the shell's one action, reachable from every tab) but it loses its lift and its pool. The CTA stays on Home because it carries the label; nothing else on Home may be lime except a measured mark.
  3. Hero numerals torn apart. "60 …… 151" at 56 pt at opposite edges, with "km/h" at 15 pt under the right one. A range is ONE value in every product that prints one — Apple Health's "60–151", Whoop's strain range. The axis grammar under it (the groove, the ticks) is kept; the numerals become one typographic unit with the unit beside them.
  4. The 테스트 pill. A raised 28 pt chip, two or three times per screen. The honesty rule says the mark must be visible everywhere fixture data is shown or shared; it does not say it must be a control. It becomes a tag: 11 pt text3, hairline border, once per card.
  5. Chart numerals inside the plot. 기록's weekly column stacks "151" and "60" over its own bar. Production charts label above the bar or on an axis, never through the mark.
  6. Truncated segment labels ("오버…" on 슛). A label that does not fit its control is a layout that was never tried at its real width.
  7. Section titles at display size (기록's "세션" at 28 pt, same as the screen title). One title per screen; sections are 17/600.
  8. Ground gradients behind headers (05-shot's horizon glow bleeding into the header). Chrome is flat. Light belongs in the picture only.

In the picture (the Codex lane, 01-BRIEF-3D.md):

  1. The hologram. Every material in src/render/ is MeshBasicMaterial — unlit — and the scene has no light, no shadow, no sky. The goal is a cyan wireframe, the net is 1 px lines, the turf is near-black, the keeper is an additive "hologram" with a halo duplicate. That is the Tron/cyberpunk trope that generated imagery converges on, and it is the opposite of the reference the founder kept: docs/design/reference/r6-journey.png is a **floodlit night pitch — green grass with mown stripes, white posts, a white net, a real ball, stadium light in the haze, and ONE lime arc**. The picture in r6 is photographic; the ink on it is graphic. That split is the direction.

What is NOT being reopened

The honesty rules (v3 §8) all stand. A realistic scene changes what "light" means — environment light claims nothing, it falls on everything equally — so §8 rule 6 is narrowed, not dropped: it binds lime emissive light (arc, ball, pool) to the band's weight as before, and it does not bind floodlights, sky or shadow. The axis grammar, gallery-gate.test.ts, the closed Lucide vocabulary and the 테스트 mark all stay. What changes is dosage and staging, not the claims.

References consulted (brands, not mockups)

  • Strava — flat orange record disc in a 5-item bar, no lift, no glow; cards with 1 px hairlines; one accent per screen.
  • Nike Run Club — a single black-on-volt CTA; numerals in one line with a small unit; generous 24 pt section gaps.
  • Whoop / Oura — ranges printed as a–b unit in one weight; charts label above bars; 11 pt uppercase overlines for metadata.
  • Apple Fitness / Health — 17/600 section headers, 13 pt captions, 10 pt tab labels under 25 pt glyphs, a 49 pt bar with no decoration.
  • TrackMan / Rapsodo (shot tracers) — photographic pitch, a single saturated tracer line, white goal; data lives in a card BELOW the picture, never floating on it.
