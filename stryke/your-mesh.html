What the new render still will not claim
  • The shape is an estimate, not a scan. SMPL-X fits body proportions to video. Your motion being real does not make the proportions measured, and the caption keeps the two apart.
  • The body steps at 7 meshes/second, not 52.6. The export is every 8th frame (plus contact), so the renderer holds each solved mesh instead of blending between them. That is a measurement, not taste: frames 64 and 72 are 8 apart and frame 71 sits inside that gap, so blending 64→72 can be checked against the mesh that was actually solved at 71 — it is off by up to 0.315 m, 17% of body height, at the exact instant of contact. Interpolating would have invented a pose you never held, so the clip steps and says it steps.
  • There is no ball. Not a faint one, not a borrowed one. This clip's ball speed is null, so nothing in the frame touches the ball and the caption says so.
  • It cannot ship. SMPL-X is research-licensed, so this is a development artifact — burnt into the frame, not just written here.

03Straight answers

"Can we avoid using the ball radius?" — mostly, yesFor the designed capture (goal in frame, ball struck from the ground), the radius is already statistically almost irrelevant: the Fisher analysis puts the plane-and-goal routes at ~2.6% on speed with the radius contributing next to nothing — the ground plane is 13.5× tighter per pixel of noise, because its lever arm is the camera height (~1.5 m) rather than the ball's 0.11 m. The radius keeps exactly two jobs it cannot be fired from: it is the only witness against a wrong goal size (the +46%/+144% guard works through it), and it is one of the few depth hints left for a ball that never touches the ground. So: demoted, not deleted.
"I just want to see the body mesh" — it never needed the ballPose recovery and ball solving are independent lanes. The mesh above came from a clip whose ball speed is null. What the mesh lane does need is a GPU (it is a minutes-per-clip job on a GPU box; this Mac has no CUDA and no GVHMR checkout), a close, well-lit subject — and your 5 s clip is exactly that. The long 1024×576 training video is the opposite: distant, small, blurred figures, which is poor input for body recovery for the same reason it is hard for the ball.
