The Pipeline

Every stage between a phone pointing at a ball and a number on screen — what each one does, which ones are built, and which ones are the reason your training footage does not work yet. Written to be argued with.

Status as of 31 Aug 2026. Anything marked built has run on a real clip; anything else says plainly what it is.

01The flow

Left column is the stage number. The chain runs top to bottom; a stage that refuses stops the chain rather than passing a guess down it.

1

Capture built

On-device recording with a rolling buffer. A kick is detected from its audio transient, and the clip is cut around it — one second before, two and a half after.

Writes shot_<n>.mov plus a shot_<n>.json sidecar carrying the real camera intrinsics, the presentation timestamp of every frame, and a device-attitude track. See §3 — this is the single biggest accuracy lever we have.

stryke-capture/ios/{CaptureController,ShotDetector,SegmentRing,ClipCutter,SidecarTracks}.swift

2

Offline shot detection & auto-cut built

An imported file now has a path: two visual proposal lanes (an audio lane was built, measured useless on real footage — 94–100% coverage across a 27-point sweep — and deleted with its evidence kept in the docstring), unioned windows, and every window resolved by the real solver into ACCEPTED / BAND_ONLY / REFUSED / NOT_A_SHOT. Proven on the 158-second real clip: 19 windows over 55.4% of the source, the one labelled shot proposed and resolved. A saturation guard measures each proposer alone and disarms any lane that proposes nearly everything.

pipeline/shots.py

3

Time-base assessment built

Speed scales 1:1 with the frame clock, so a wrong clock is a wrong speed — and re-encoded video lies about its clock constantly.

Three container rates are cross-checked against each other and against duplicate-frame ratio. Disagreement does not widen the band quietly; it refuses the point value by name. Real per-frame stamps from the sidecar upgrade this from an inference to a measurement.

ball.py · assess_timebase() · probe_container_timebase()

4

Stabilisation built

Camera shake is removed before anything is tracked: corner flow on background features, RANSAC affine per frame pair, composed into a transform to the contact frame. On-screen UI is auto-masked so a static overlay is not mistaken for static ground.

ball.py · stabilize()

5

Ball acquisition dead-ball + moving-ball

The wall. The pipeline finds the ball by first finding it at rest — a white circle that holds still, then departs. Free kicks and penalties work. Everything else raises before a single measurement is attempted.

find_rest_ball() # Hough circles + whiteness track_rest_until_departure() # template match until it leaves RuntimeError: no rest-ball candidate produced a departing track

Now a second entry strategy: a moving-ball path that finds the strike the boot hides — the ball is occluded through contact, so identity across the gap is proven by white-disc confirmation on BOTH sides (junk fails at least one side), and contact lands exactly on the labelled frame. On the real clip that used to die here: 40 observations, median 1.46 px from the hand-checked track, band emitted with the point honestly refused.

6

Impact detection just rebuilt

Finding when the ball was struck. Contact is t=0 for the whole flight fit, so an error here is an error in every number downstream.

Until today, contact came from the rest-ball departure, with a refinement that looked for the biggest jump in speed. That is correct for a dead ball and wrong for a moving one: a ball rolling in at 6 px/frame and returned at 7 px/frame has almost no speed jump and a complete reversal of direction. The new detector keys on the change in the velocity vector, which catches both with one quantity.

delta[i] = | v[i] − v[i−1] | # the impulse contact = argmax delta # ball is AT the contact point

It reports sharpness (peak over the next-best rival), prominence (peak over the track's own noise floor) and frames_uncertain, and refuses outright when no candidate stands out. The noise-floor test earned its place immediately: without it, the detector confidently reported a contact on a synthetic track where nothing was ever struck.

pipeline/impact.py — new, 11 tests passing

7

Tracking built · the strong part

Once seeded, this is genuinely good and is not being replaced. Three independent trackers run every frame and are fused per frame:

  • double frame-difference blobs, for the fast-moving frames
  • white mask + Hough circles, for the slow and sharp ones
  • optical-flow template match, for continuity through both

Plus outlier re-search against a local quadratic fit. The overlay you liked — fused dots, reprojected 3D fit — comes from here.

ball.py · track_ball() · _refit_pass() · render_overlay()

8

Metric scale: the goal is the ruler built

A camera sees pixels. Metres require one object of known real size in frame — the goal mouth, 7.32 m × 2.44 m. Its four corners plus the camera's focal length give depth through PnP.

Two consequences worth arguing about: the whole goal must be visible, and a dead-on straight shot is geometrically degenerate (a rectangle projects as a rectangle at any focal/depth pair), which is why we ask for 30–60° off the shot line.

ball.py · detect_goal() — now scans a window of frames around contact, since the kicker often occludes a post on the contact frame itself

9

Flight fit built

A physical model, not a curve through points: quadratic drag with the drag-crisis Reynolds transition, plus a Magnus term for topspin. Multiple solution basins are enumerated rather than the first one being reported as if it were unique.

ball.py · fit_flight() · integrate_flight() · DragModel

10

The band built

The product is an interval, not a midpoint dressed as a fact. It is assembled by re-solving under every perturbation we know we have, and taking the envelope.

speeds = primary + per-anchor solves (gravity / diameter / goal) + focal × 0.85 and × 1.15 # the ±15% intrinsics guess + tilt ± 5° + every surviving solution basin + the alternate model class band = [min, max] × (1 ± frame-rate uncertainty)

The next term to enter this list is contact-instant uncertainty from stage 6 — re-fitting at contact ± frames_uncertain and folding those speeds in. That is what will make a moving-ball band honestly wider than a dead-ball one.

11

Refusal built

Refusals are named, never folded into a quietly wider band. A better input may remove an error; it may never silently remove a refusal.

focal_from_diagonal_heuristic_pm15pct · camera_rotation_exceeds_compensation_limit · goal_fronto_parallel · impact_indistinguishable_from_track_noise · …

12

Keeper detection built

Classical CV, CPU, tested on the real footage: the keeper is found and his stance measured in goal-plane metres — on the long clip he stands at −2.54 m from centre, 2.5 m away from where the old constant assumed. The sunset-sky content that once fooled the goal detector is refused here. The shooter-identification half was deleted after it was caught returning the ball's own disc as "the shooter" — it now returns a standing refusal rather than a wrong box.

13

Body mesh / pose built licence-blocked

World-frame SMPL-X body recovery works and has produced real meshes. The obstacle is not code — it is that the model chain is non-commercial and cannot ship in a paid app. The fix is a licence-clean swap, not a third pose stack.

The catch, and it is a real oneAll of the above exists only for clips recorded inside the app. An imported file — anything from the Camera app, AirDrop, or a share sheet — arrives with none of it, keeps the ±15% focal warning, and gets the wider band. That is the honest cost of importing, and it is the strongest practical argument for shooting in-app once the capture path is proven on a real device.

04What is being decided right now

Ball acquisition — measured, and rejectedA learned ball detector (WASB, MIT-licensed, 6 MB, already implemented here in a CUDA-free form) looked like the obvious fix for stage 5. It was measured against our own hand-checked track before anything was wired in, and it scored zero true positives — median error 190–260 px on a 1024×576 frame. The classical ensemble gets 40/40 on the same clip.

The premise behind adopting it was wrong, and the measurement is worth keeping: the ball is 29 px only while at rest, and a median of 10 px in flight, 7.5 px at the goal. The decisive detail is that WASB found the 6 px stationary spare ball and missed the 40 px foreground one. Ball size was never the binding constraint — motion blur is.
And moving-ball support is not a detector swapThe resting ball is load-bearing in three places, not one: the track seed, the sharpest radius measurement (the launch-depth anchor), and the launch height — the launch point is placed on the ground, and a ball already moving has no known height at t=0. Bolting on any detector does not restore the same quantity. What a moving-ball clip may honestly claim is a decision to make before writing the code, not after.
Closed since this page was writtenTwo ways a confident wrong speed could reach you, both now shut:

The focal band ignored its own provenance. The ±15% perturbation is the diagonal heuristic's error, and it ran unconditionally — so a clip that handed us the camera's real intrinsic matrix still paid the guess's penalty. A band that does not narrow when the input improves has stopped tracking the evidence.

The declared goal size was never checked. A wrong goal size does not produce a bad fit; it produces a perfect fit at the wrong depth, because a rectangle's absolute size is unobservable from one view. A 5 m 7-a-side goal declared as regulation is +46% on every speed, a 3 m futsal goal +144%, with nothing in the solve objecting. The one witness is the ball's own 22 cm — and it was already being polled, its verdict printed as a warning beside a confident point speed. Past 35% disagreement the point value is now refused.
The accuracy stack, as of 1 SepFour fixes landed in sequence, each measured:

The fit no longer welds to the contact frame (was ±13–16% per frame of contact error). The band no longer publishes solver bounds as measurements — the recurring 248.4 km/h ceiling was the v0 parameter bound × the timing uncertainty; two reference bands narrowed from 4.4× and 13.6× wide to 1.9× and 2.7×. The goal's pose is no longer thrown away — solvePnP's rotation gives camera height and tilt (the dominant error, 6–8% per degree), with width propagated from the corner noise, refusing itself when the goal is square-on. Wrong-goal declarations now get CAUGHT HARDER: with height pinned, a size lie has nowhere to hide but the ruler comparison (spread 0.2% → 45.7%). Result on a synthetic angled-goal clip (truth 54.0): band 30.2% wide square-on → 5.3% at 25° → 3.0% at 40°, truth inside every band. The 30–60° capture rule is no longer advice — it is the measured price of a tight band.
What still stands between this and a ±10 claimReal corners are noisier than synthetic ones, and no measurement here has ever been checked against an external reference. Two steps, neither of them code: run the designed capture on a real pitch, and one session against a radar gun. The band is allowed to be the answer until then.

05The shortest honest summary

The middle of the pipeline — tracking, physics, geometry, the band, the refusals — is in good shape and is not the problem. Both ends are.

  • The front end cannot find shots in a video you already have, and cannot start from a ball that is already moving. That is why your training footage does not work.
  • The far end does not see the keeper, so anything about reach or coverage is currently an assumption wearing a number.
  • The accuracy ceiling is now set by the contact-anchored fit above, and by the fact that no measurement here has yet been checked against a radar. The focal length is fixed; the goal size is guarded.
